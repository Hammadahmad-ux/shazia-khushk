# UX Reference Benchmark

## Scope and Method

Reviewed 9 August 2026 using current official home, collection, and PDP surfaces from [COS](https://www.cos.com/en-us/women), [Zara](https://www.zara.com/ww/), [ARKET](https://www.arket.com/en-gb/), [Aesop](https://www.aesop.com/skin-care.html), [Glossier](https://www.glossier.com/products/glossier-you), [Rare Beauty](https://www.rarebeauty.com/collections/beauty-products), [Le Labo](https://www.lelabofragrances.com/santal-33-147.html?size=50ml), and [Byredo](https://www.byredo.com/us_en/c/perfume/personal-fragrances/view-all-collection). These are behavioral references only. No brand identity, copy, composition, assets, or proprietary layout should be copied.

The evaluation favors a small Pakistani multi-category catalog, mobile purchasing, explicit variants, and honest commerce over features built for global inventory scale.

## COS

**Strongest patterns**

- Compact mobile header keeps logo, search, account, bag, and menu available without a mega-header.
- Collection entry combines breadcrumb/title, one filter-and-sort control, and optional grid-density controls.
- Homepage/department surfaces use one dominant campaign followed by direct category entrances rather than placing a product grid above every story.
- Product presentation is image-led; metadata is secondary and consistently aligned.

**Weak or unnecessary here**

- Account entry and multiple grid-density modes add controls without improving a small catalog.
- Large global fashion taxonomies and sale navigation would overbuild our header.
- Extremely sparse space before products can delay discovery on short mobile sessions.

**Apply:** compact header, editorial category entrances, image-first collection cards, combined filter/sort trigger.

**Do not apply:** account-first navigation, dense department trees, user-controlled grid density, sale-led structure.

## Zara

**Strongest patterns**

- Campaign-led discovery treats a collection as a visual story instead of an inventory database.
- Strong category naming and full-look merchandising create decisive entry points.
- Persistent access to search and bag keeps commerce reachable during immersive browsing.

**Weak or unnecessary here**

- Multi-level navigation contains collections, editorial, size shortcuts, projects, and many product types; it demands learned navigation.
- Full-screen visual storytelling can hide context and make backtracking harder.
- Rapid campaign turnover and high inventory assumptions do not suit a small managed catalog.

**Apply:** decisive editorial entrances and visual product grouping.

**Do not apply:** numbered mega-navigation, opaque scroll journeys, full-look inventory scale, or hidden product context.

## ARKET

**Strongest patterns**

- Product, craft, and editorial content coexist without making every content block a sales card.
- “Discover more” links let customers enter adjacent stories after core merchandising.
- Calm hierarchy supports multiple consumer categories while retaining a coherent brand voice.

**Weak or unnecessary here**

- Women, men, children, homeware, café, and editorial archives create breadth our catalog does not need.
- Long brand/editorial archives are expensive to maintain without a content team.

**Apply:** considered brand-story placement, category context, and clear secondary discovery.

**Do not apply:** lifestyle-ecosystem navigation or a publication program at launch.

## Aesop

**Strongest patterns**

- Collection pages combine products with need-based paths such as skin type/concern, making education part of discovery rather than a separate help center.
- Product cards include a concise “for whom/what” descriptor and direct add action for simple products.
- Assistance, regimen guidance, and relevant reading appear after core product discovery.
- Trust/support information is placed near the end of the journey and in a structured footer.

**Weak or unnecessary here**

- Skin finders, live consultations, long educational archives, and several product rails require content/operations we do not yet have.
- Multiple horizontal carousels make keyboard/mobile comparison harder than a short stable list.
- Large global region and consent layers add entry friction.

**Apply:** category-specific guidance, concise suitability descriptors, education after products, verified support paths.

**Do not apply:** diagnostic tools, live consultancy, content-heavy carousels, or health/suitability claims without evidence.

## Glossier

**Strongest patterns**

- PDP variant choices are explicit radio-like controls; volume, price, and add action stay together.
- Fragrance PDP progresses from identity and purchase into sensory story, gallery, scent notes, ingredients, reviews, and relevant products.
- Mobile header uses a simple menu/search/bag triad.
- The cart drawer has a clear heading, close action, shipping-progress context, empty-state message, and direct shop recovery.
- Cards can expose a small number of variants and direct add without forcing a PDP visit.

**Weak or unnecessary here**

- Subscription options, engraving, several bundle rails, extensive recommendations, and thousands of reviews overload the purchase narrative.
- Preselecting the largest volume can produce an expensive accidental default.
- Variant-rich cards become noisy when many shades/options are displayed.
- Free-shipping progress can feel promotional if the client has not approved the threshold.

**Apply:** grouped variant/price/CTA, category-specific PDP story, accessible gallery controls, cart recovery.

**Do not apply:** subscriptions, bundle builders, engraving, large review systems, or silent high-price defaults.

## Rare Beauty

**Strongest patterns**

- Collection-level category navigation is visible before the grid.
- Swatches update product context, and add/out-of-stock actions clearly reflect availability.
- Homepage campaigns provide direct routes into product families, shade guidance, and community content.
- Product status is stated in the action (“Add to Bag,” “Out of Stock,” “Coming Soon”) rather than hidden in decoration.

**Weak or unnecessary here**

- Promotional announcements, gifts, sale badges, exclusives, shade counts, bundles, and chat can compete for attention.
- Large swatch sets make cards interaction-heavy and unsuitable for a small cross-category assortment.
- Community modules and impact claims require real programs, moderation, and evidence.

**Apply:** explicit status/action states and category-aware card options when the option set is small.

**Do not apply:** promotional stacking, shade-grid cards, floating chat by default, or unsupported community/impact claims.

## Le Labo

**Strongest patterns**

- PDP purchase hierarchy is clear: fragrance/form, size, quantity, optional personalization, delivery information, then story and ingredients.
- Scent storytelling uses a concise narrative before technical disclosure.
- Ingredient content is available without crowding the initial purchase area.
- Recommendations remain within the scent/product family.

**Weak or unnecessary here**

- The global menu exposes many scents, formats, refills, home, grooming, gifts, and location-specific services.
- Personalization, engraving, store pickup, and location gates are operationally complex.
- Quantity selectors at the primary decision point add friction for products normally bought one at a time.

**Apply:** fragrance-specific hierarchy, nearby delivery reassurance, progressive ingredient disclosure, tightly related recommendations.

**Do not apply:** personalization, refills, store pickup, global location gating, or large scent-by-format navigation.

## Byredo

**Strongest patterns**

- Collection intro provides fragrance context before a restrained grid.
- Filters and grid controls are visually secondary to product imagery.
- Cards communicate format, fragrance name, price, and whether additional sizes exist.
- Discovery tools are inserted as editorial service moments rather than dashboard widgets.

**Weak or unnecessary here**

- Wishlist, “try it first,” fragrance finder, and filtering are justified by roughly 80 products, not our small catalog.
- “50 ml +2 sizes” summarizes availability but can conceal actual price/volume differences.
- A large intro/hero can push the first product too far below the mobile fold.

**Apply:** concise collection context, restrained product metadata, and optional service content after discovery.

**Do not apply:** wishlist at launch, trial programs, finder tools, or inventory-scale filtering.

## KEEP

- A compact header with persistent search and cart access; category links remain direct and shallow.
- One image-led campaign/collection story followed by immediate category or product choices.
- Product cards with stable 4:5 media space, name, short category-relevant descriptor, PKR price, factual state, and one action.
- Explicit size/color/volume controls adjacent to price and add-to-cart on the PDP.
- Product-specific information hierarchy: material/fit/care; scent/volume/notes; ingredients/use/warnings.
- Delivery, COD/payment, and returns summaries placed beside purchase decisions and linked to full policies.
- Mobile bag/cart as a focused sheet with clear close, subtotal, checkout, and empty-state recovery.
- Need-based storytelling after core products when source facts exist.

## ADAPT

- Use quick-add only for one active variant. For required choices, open a small accessible selector sheet or route to the PDP.
- Use category navigation as plain text links/tabs; avoid oversized mega-menu taxonomies.
- Keep one curated product edit on the homepage, then use story-led category modules instead of repeated grids.
- Show only filters that can materially reduce the current result set; hide the filter control when no useful filter exists.
- Use one second product image on hover/focus where approved media exists; on touch, keep all essential information/action visible.
- Offer a sticky mobile purchase bar only after the main purchase controls scroll away; missing variants convert it into “Choose size/volume.”
- Use reviews/social proof only when verified data, rights, moderation, and enough volume exist.
- Treat WhatsApp/help as contextual recovery, not a floating control covering mobile commerce.

## AVOID

- Copying any reference brand’s logo placement, typography, palette, campaign composition, wording, or asset treatment.
- Megamenus, wishlist/account requirements, subscriptions, personalization, discovery quizzes, store pickup, or content archives without a launch requirement.
- Defaulting customers into an expensive or required variant; every material choice must be visible.
- Horizontal carousels as the only route to categories/products, or swipe-only gallery navigation.
- Multiple product rails, recommendation walls, shade matrices, bundle builders, and review blocks on one PDP.
- Sale/countdown/free-shipping messaging without approved rules and server-backed facts.
- Large editorial intros that prevent a mobile shopper from reaching products or a purchase action quickly.
- Decorative density controls, badges, icons, and floating support widgets that compete with the primary task.
